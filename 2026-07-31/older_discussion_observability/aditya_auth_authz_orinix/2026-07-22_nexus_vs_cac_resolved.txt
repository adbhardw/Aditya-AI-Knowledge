RESOLVED: NEXUS AND CAC ARE NOT TWO SEPARATE SYSTEMS — CAC IS AN APP INSIDE NEXUS
Follow-up to 2026-07-22_keys_service_accounts_why_nexus_still_needed.txt.
This corrects sloppy phrasing from my previous answer that caused confusion.
Date: 2026-07-22

DIRECT EVIDENCE: LiveRamp/nexus is a MONOREPO. Its `apps/` directory
contains, side by side:

    access-control        <- THIS IS CAC. Confirmed directly:
                              apps/access-control/emulator/README.md literally
                              opens with "# Central Access Control (CAC) API
                              Emulator"
    service-account        <- issues tokens, holds/rotates signing keys,
                               serves JWKS (the diagram from the previous doc)
    org-api
    idp-snapshots
    internal-okta-to-cac-syncer
    liveramp-token-fetcher
    self-signup-service
    third-party-app-sso-gateway
    thirdshield (+ thirdshield-cli, thirdshield-hackui)
    v2sa-mapping-backfill
    ...and a few more

So: CAC is not a separate company system that Nexus happens to depend on.
CAC (access-control) is literally one app living inside Nexus, as a sibling
to the service-account app. Nexus is the umbrella platform/monorepo for
ALL of LiveRamp's identity and access management — authentication AND
authorization both live under it, as two of its several apps.


===========================================================
Q&A — YOUR EXACT QUESTIONS
===========================================================

Q1: Is Nexus the authn service for the sidecar-based approach, or the
    CAC-based approach too, or am I completely wrong?

A1: Not wrong — just resolved now. There is only ONE authn system,
    Nexus's service-account app, used by BOTH approaches identically. Every
    token, in either approach, is issued and signed by the same place
    (Nexus). The "sidecar vs CAC-based" difference was never about who
    ISSUES the token — it's only about who VERIFIES the signature on the
    way in: the receiving service's own local authn-proxy sidecar (sidecar
    approach), or CAC itself as part of its permission check (CAC-only
    approach, e.g. MCS). Both verification paths ultimately check the
    signature against the same Nexus-issued signing key — one does it
    locally with a cached public key, the other does it as part of a call
    to CAC, which is itself a Nexus app.

Q3: Is a service account created for both MCS and VMA, and is the
    credentials.json stored in Vault used to authenticate to Nexus or to
    CAC?

A3: Yes — MCS and VMA each have their own separate service account (each
    needs its own identity, so CAC can grant/scope permissions to one
    without touching the other). The credentials.json is used to
    authenticate to NEXUS specifically (the service-account app's
    "Obtain Access Token" endpoint) — not to CAC directly. Sequence: app's
    access-token-getter sidecar reads credentials.json -> presents it to
    Nexus's service-account/Authenticator -> gets back a short-lived signed
    access token -> THAT token (not the credentials file) is what later gets
    sent to CAC, or to any other LiveRamp API, as the Authorization header.
    CAC never sees the credentials.json at all.

Q4: Is CAC separate from Nexus, or does CAC use Nexus (or is it the
    reverse)?

A4: Resolved above — neither "separate" nor "uses" is quite right. CAC IS
    Nexus, in the sense that it's one of Nexus's own apps, not an external
    dependency. They're not two systems calling each other across a company
    boundary; they're two apps in the same platform/team's monorepo.

Q5 (auth flow for orinix): "my understanding for orinix is it will have a
    service account and this SA is authenticated by CAC using credential.json,
    and then CAC will verify the JWT using the private/public key in Google
    Secret Manager" — is this right?

A5: Close, but one hop is misplaced — worth correcting precisely:
    credentials.json authenticates orinix's SA to NEXUS's service-account
    app (not to CAC) to OBTAIN a token in the first place. That token —
    already signed, already representing "this is orinix" — is what then
    gets sent onward to CAC (or to XMI's endpoint, which itself may call
    CAC) for the PERMISSION check. CAC checking the permission likely does
    involve validating that token's signature too (same signing key, since
    CAC is a sibling Nexus app and can reuse the same verification
    machinery) — but the credentials.json itself never goes anywhere near
    CAC. It's: credentials.json -> Nexus (get token) -> token -> CAC
    (check permission, which also implicitly validates the token) -- not
    credentials.json -> CAC directly.

Q5 (second question, "does this mean Nexus sits for authz too, since CAC
    comes under it?"):

A5b: Yes — exactly right, and this is the actual resolution to the whole
    confusion. Nexus is not just the authn (identity/token) system — it's
    the umbrella platform that owns BOTH authn (service-account app) AND
    authz (access-control/CAC app) as sibling components of the same
    platform. "Nexus" and "CAC" aren't two different systems in a
    relationship with each other — CAC is one specific app that lives
    inside Nexus. Saying "CAC" is really just naming the authz-specific
    piece of the broader Nexus platform, the same way you'd say "MCS's
    Celery worker" while still meaning it's part of MCS overall.
